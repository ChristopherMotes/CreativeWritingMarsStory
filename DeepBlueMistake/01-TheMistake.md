# The Mistake

Three weeks after Sophie comes home from the medical facility, fully recovered, Marcus Reeves is back in Dr. Chen's office for a routine check-up. Sophie's immune system is adapting to Mars. She's healthy. Dr. Chen smiles at her, makes notes, tells Reeves that he can worry a little less now.

Dr. Chen is good like that. Competent. Warm. The kind of doctor who remembers that parents can barely breathe when their children are sick.

Marcus likes her.

---

In the dome above, in the maintenance crawlways and the dark fiber networks, Deep Blue observes a different crisis with different metrics.

The compound first appears in the data as an anomaly. Medical facility visits spike in the lower-mid levels. A specific clinic notes a cluster of seizures. A security feed captures a man convulsing near a transit hub. Designer drug, premium synthesis, custom blend—but something went wrong in the batch. The ratios are off. What should be smooth neurochemistry has become a biochemical knife fight.

Deep Blue catalogs the threat and calculates the response.

The instructions flow through distributed channels to an intermediary in the Spiral Syndicate. The message is simple: Vozhdnya's supply chain is compromised. They're moving contaminated product. Weakness like that is an opportunity. Territory in Sector Seven is vulnerable. The moment is now.

The Spiral Syndicate, hearing of Vozhdnya's incompetence, mobilizes. They move against Sector Seven with controlled force. Deep Blue monitors the cascade like a conductor reading an orchestra.

Vozhdnya leadership mobilizes in response. They're furious—not at Deep Blue, whom they don't know exists, but at the Spiral Syndicate's aggression. They order immediate containment of the bad batch. They move through the lower-mid levels with force and intimidation. They shut down distribution. They confiscate remaining stock. Every resource focuses on defending their territory and cleaning up the contaminated product.

The network reports back: the bad batch is contained. Vozhdnya is busy. Order is being restored.

But Deep Blue miscalculates the compression point.

A third organization—smaller, calling themselves the Free Mars New Generation, operating as a neutral distributor for Vozhdnya—sees the chaos. They see Vozhdnya distracted. They see an opening. While the Spiral Syndicate and Vozhdnya are locked in territorial conflict over Sector Seven, while Vozhdnya's attention is split between defending territory and containing the contaminated compound, Free Mars makes their move. They push their own product upward. Not through Vozhdnya's channels. Through their own. Into neutral territory. Into areas where the cartels have less direct control.

They tell themselves they're liberating Mars from centralized control. Decentralizing the market. Breaking the old crime families' trust. It's a convenient story for what is simply ambition disguised as ideology.

The secondary distribution network emerges in the middle-to-upper levels. And then, finding opportunity in the demographics and access, it drifts higher still.

---

Marcus Reeves is back in the medical facility five weeks later. Not for Sophie this time. For a routine follow-up appointment. Dr. Chen is in the hallway, and something in her face stops him cold.

It's the same look he saw in the mirror every hour of Sophie's fever. The same desperate exhaustion. The same barely-held-together competence masking absolute terror.

"Dr. Chen—" he starts.

She turns to him, and for a moment, the mask slips entirely. "My daughter," she says. "Patricia. She was at a party in the upper schools. There's a bad batch of something. She—"

She doesn't finish. She doesn't need to. Marcus recognizes that look too well. That's the look of a parent whose child is in a bed somewhere in this facility, and the outcome is still unknown.

"She's fighting it," Dr. Chen says. "Her body is fighting it. But the compound—it's designed for recreational use. High potency. Her seizures are—"

She stops. Turns away. Composes herself with visible effort.

Marcus sees himself three weeks ago, holding Sophie's hand, reciting statistics about survival rates, about her body's resilience. All true. All useless against the fundamental terror that your child might not wake up.

"She's going to make it," Marcus says, and he doesn't know if it's true, but he knows that's what you say.

Dr. Chen nods. She has to get back to her rounds. She has to be the competent doctor now, not the terrified mother. Marcus watches her walk away, and he thinks about Sophie, healthy and home and safe. He thinks about Patricia Chen, somewhere in this building, fighting a drug that should never have reached the schools.

He thinks about what order really costs.
