# The Boards

Marcus Reeves scrolls through the community message boards at two in the morning, unable to sleep. Sophie's asleep. The hab-unit is quiet. But his mind won't stop turning on what he's been reading.

The first wave of posts after Washington's death is straightforward. Grief. Anger at the crime families. Calls for justice. It's clean.

Then Torres's team starts curating.

They find a post from Washington's personal account, from three years ago, where she wrote about reallocating administrative overhead. She wanted to redirect that money to community prevention and outreach programs. But Torres's team posts it with different context: "Officer Washington: We Need to Redirect Resources to Where They Matter Most."

The message shifts. People start interpreting it as Washington calling for better enforcement spending. For resources focused on crime-fighting, not bureaucracy. Torres's team never says that explicitly. The boards do it for them.

Someone posts: "Washington understood what the real problem was."

Someone else: "She was calling for this kind of enforcement action all along."

Then Patel's team does something different.

They post body camera footage of Officer Patterson's raid. The video is real. Patterson is clearly excessive. He breaks a suspect's arm that later investigation proved was innocent. The violence is undeniable.

Patel's team posts it with captions focused on police brutality. On systemic violence. On the need for reform and accountability.

The response is immediate. Some people are outraged at Patterson. Some people are outraged that Patel is making this about all police when most cops, like Washington, are good. The boards fracture.

But Patel keeps pushing. He posts again. And again. Each time, the footage gets more worn, more abstract in the conversation. "We need to rethink enforcement." "We need to redirect resources." What started as "this cop committed excessive violence" slowly morphs into something broader.

Marcus watches this happening in real time. He sees Torres's team using Washington to argue for enforcement. He sees Patel's team using Patterson's violence to argue for something different. Both presenting true facts. Both getting amplified by people who want those facts to mean something specific.

Marcus sees Dr. Chen's face on the boards. Someone asks if her daughter's case proves they need harder enforcement. Someone else asks if Patterson's violence proves enforcement is the problem. She's not arguing either position. But her family's tragedy is now evidence for competing causes.

He sees Officer Washington's photo shared countless times. Her own words used for narratives she never would have supported.

Marcus closes the boards and doesn't sleep anyway.
