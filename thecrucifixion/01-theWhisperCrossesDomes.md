# The Whisper

The Whisper had crossed into Secondshima and perched quietly above a crowd, hidden in the dark frame of an open window. The building stood between the night-life district and the older residential blocks, where the music and bright signage gave way to food stalls, repair shops, and late-running trams. From the street, the open window looked empty. The Whisper's matte-black armor merged with the unlit room behind him; only the reflective visor caught a faint, broken glimmer when he shifted his head.

He read the short note glowing across his augmented-reality visor: JEFFERY HINTON — SUSPECT.

The Whisper had to be careful in Secondshima. Police were rare, but the populace was armed, and people did not always hide it. Some men carried pistols openly. Most carried concealed, as did many women. Here, a weapon was not an exotic sight or an automatic invitation to panic. It was simply part of the crowd, like a work bag or a set of keys. The Whisper was practiced at moving unseen, but he could not mistake concealment for safety.

Below him, the market shifted around its own small currents. Night-shift workers crossed paths with people just leaving the clubs. A vendor leaned over a steaming pan while a customer counted change. Two armed women in jackets passed a group of men arguing over a tram delay; no one stopped talking, and no one reached for a weapon. A delivery cart squeezed through the gap between them. The crowd parted, closed, and carried on.

The Whisper watched Hinton through it all without drawing attention. His helmet's sensors separated voices from music and street noise, isolating Hinton's words as he spoke to another man.

“Yes,” Hinton said. “One thirteen, one nine, and of course my daughter. She's the prize.”

The Whisper had what he needed. He could deliver the recording to Jimmy Nuts. Nuts could arrest Hinton the next time he crossed into Muskashima, where he could be rehabilitated and returned to society as a healthy, productive man.

That was not Hinton's path.

Something small moved against the market's lights: a dark shape, no larger than a bird, holding too steady in the air to be one. A red status point blinked once beneath its shell. The Whisper's sensors marked it as an AI monitoring drone. It had slipped past the outer systems and found him.

The drone was not from Secondshima Police or any Secondshima government agency. It belonged to the Scarlet Mask.

The Whisper moved from the window and into the crowded passage below. Matte-black composite plates, layered in the segmented shape of traditional armor, covered his chest and shoulders. Charcoal panels broke up the black, while dark synthetic material sealed the joints and hid the circuitry beneath. The seams carried fine grey piping, functional lines for the armor's power and data systems. A reinforced emblem on his breastplate showed Mars encircled by falcon wings.

His full-face visor reflected the market back at itself: faces, lights, and weapons bending across its glossy black surface. The same falcon-wing crest rose from his helmet, its narrow extensions serving as antennae for his communications and sensors. Broad-shouldered but balanced, he moved without hurry. Each step was economical, each turn deliberate. He did not shove through the crowd or ask it to make way. People simply found themselves giving the dark, silent figure room.

The drone followed, keeping him in view as he crossed beneath the signs and out toward the transit arcade. Its red point blinked again in the visor's reflection.
