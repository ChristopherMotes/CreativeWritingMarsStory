# The Whisper

The Whisper had crossed into Secondshima and perched quietly above a crowd, hidden in the dark frame of an open window. The building stood between the night-life district and the older residential blocks, where the music and bright signage gave way to food stalls, repair shops, and late-running trams. From the street, the open window looked empty. The Whisper's matte-black armor merged with the unlit room behind him; only the reflective visor caught a faint, broken glimmer when he shifted his head.

He read the short note glowing across his augmented-reality visor: JEFFERY HINTON — SUSPECT.

The Whisper had to be careful in Secondshima. Police were rare, but the populace was armed, and people did not always hide it. Some men carried pistols openly. Most carried them concealed, as did many women. Here, a weapon was not an exotic sight or an automatic invitation to panic. It was simply part of the crowd, like a work bag or a set of keys. The Whisper was practiced at moving unseen, but he could not mistake concealment for safety.

Below him, the market shifted around its own small currents. Night-shift workers crossed paths with people just leaving the clubs. A vendor leaned over a steaming pan while a customer counted change. Two armed women in jackets passed a group of men arguing over a tram delay; no one stopped talking, and no one reached for a weapon. A delivery cart squeezed through the gap between them. The crowd parted, closed, and carried on.

The Whisper watched Hinton through it all without drawing attention. His helmet's sensors separated voices from music and street noise, isolating Hinton's words as he spoke to another man.

“Yes,” Hinton said. “One thirteen, one nine, and of course my daughter. She's the prize.”

The Whisper had what he needed. He could deliver the recording to Jimmy Nuts. Nuts could arrest Hinton the next time he crossed into Muskashima, where he could be rehabilitated and returned to society as a healthy, productive man.

That was not Hinton's path.

Something small moved against the market's lights: a dark shape, no larger than a bird, holding too steady in the air to be one. A red status point blinked once beneath its shell. The Whisper's sensors marked it as an AI monitoring drone. It had slipped past the outer systems and found him.

The drone was not from Secondshima Police or any Secondshima government agency. It belonged to the Scarlet Mask.

The Whisper moved from the window and into the crowded passage below. Matte-black composite plates, layered in the segmented shape of traditional armor, covered his chest and shoulders. Charcoal panels broke up the black, while dark synthetic material sealed the joints and hid the circuitry beneath. The seams carried fine grey piping, functional lines for the armor's power and data systems. A reinforced emblem on his breastplate showed Mars encircled by falcon wings.

His full-face visor reflected the market back at itself: faces, lights, and weapons bending across its glossy black surface. The same falcon-wing crest rose from his helmet, its narrow extensions serving as antennae for his communications and sensors. Broad-shouldered but balanced, he moved without hurry. Each step was economical, each turn deliberate. He did not shove through the crowd or ask it to make way. People simply found themselves giving the dark, silent figure room.

The drone followed, keeping him in view as he crossed beneath the signs and out toward the transit arcade. Its red point blinked again in the visor's reflection.

“Mask,” Monk broke through on the emergency channel. “I've got eyes on Whisper in Secondshima. He's moving out,” he continued. “It's bad, boss. He's crowd-adjacent. I couldn't put a shot on him if you asked. Bad shot, through and through. Too many variables. Maybe we should let him go.”

Stevie Tanner—the business name Tanaka used because the world believed Stevie Tanaka was dead—sat in a corporate office owned by one of the shell companies funding the Scarlet Mask's operation. Of all the duties that came with the mask, the executive work was the one he hated. Intelligence analysis? Yes. Combat action? Absolutely. Leading good men? Every day. But the paperwork, the schedules, the meetings: that part of the job made him feel like his head belonged on the desk.

It nearly landed there when his MCD cracked with Monk's voice.

Circuits cut into the shared channel. “I've got a dark area we can move him to. Near all of us. He fucked up bad. It'll take us longer to load up than to get there. Just say go, boss.”

Circuits sent the location to Stevie's MCD.

“Monk, lights and threats only. I don't want an ounce of civilian blood spilled. If you get a shot, brain him. Otherwise, wait for reinforcements,” Stevie ordered.

“Circuits, Washington, Whisper is no joke. Full standard tac squad. Move out. My ETA is six minutes.”

Circuits launched the drones as he ran to the ACO combat vehicle. Shorter and denser than most of the Men of the Mask, he carried the compact posture of a Marine despite a career spent in technical spaces. His regulation-short dark hair was graying at the temples; thick-rimmed glasses sat on his narrow nose. Even at a run, his eyes measured distance, timing, and risk. His driver and operator were waiting.

The force was organized into three elements: Scouting, Deception, and Maneuver; Command and Control; and Advanced Combat Operations. Circuits led ACO-A. Washington led C&C-B. C&C-A had an empty seat where the Scarlet Mask should have been.

The air-light-cavalry drones were larger than the monitoring drone that had first spotted the Whisper. They lifted into the night, dark shapes with sensor heads turning beneath them. They could track a moving target and keep it in sight. Circuits sent them after the Whisper.

Monk lay prone in the shadow of a high Secondshima building, a sniper rifle settled beside him. He was tall and narrow, his long frame shaped by a lifetime in Martian gravity. Pale eyes watched through the optic beneath close-cropped hair already thinning at the temples. Beside him, his spotter lay still in the standard dark uniform and helmet of the Men of the Mask.

Monk made no effort to hide his rifle's optics from the Whisper. He wanted the Whisper to notice him.

Circuits and Monk worked to draw the Whisper away from the crowd and toward an isolated stretch of the city.

Stevie sprinted down the street. His Martian-born frame carried the dense strength he had built by surviving full Earth-gravity training; his Japanese ancestry showed only faintly in his features. He wore an overcoat and a medium-sized sling bag, with a Martian Issue Service Rifle secured to the outside. In Secondshima, the rifle drew little attention; most men carried a weapon, though pistols were more common. He carried the Scarlet Mask's augmented-reality combat helmet, scarlet with gold tiger striping. When he donned it, Stevie became the Scarlet Mask: a generational legacy, more than any one man.

He tracked the Whisper on his MCD. *Fuck,* he thought. *He's moving us as much as we're moving him. The fucker is negotiating terrain.*

Monk broke in over the secured channel. “We gotta hold the fucker there. I lose visual on the next street.”

As always, things got worse.

In Muskashima, the Whisper was often treated as a hero. He addressed gaps in the legal system, taking down bad cops so good cops could do their jobs. For many—perhaps most—he was the glue that held Muskashima together. To some, he was a dangerous criminal like any other. To the rest, he was a necessary but tragic part of a healthy system.

Secondshimamen saw him differently. To them, the Whisper was a symbol of government domestic espionage—a leader of a new Gestapo. They hated him as much as Stevie Tanaka did.

Like the Men of the Mask, Secondshima's militiamen knew how to handle weapons safely. With the crowd around them, they kept their pistols holstered and used debris and words to make their displeasure clear.

“Fuck you, you goddamned tyrant!” one militiaman shouted, throwing a broken length of pipe. He wore shorts and flip-flops and a T-shirt printed with a Martian Issue Service Rifle and the words, “For defending homes, not profits.”

“Let Leo Musk know we don't want his Orwellian nightmare here!” another shouted, punctuating the words with an obscene gesture. The man wore a disco suit and openly carried a high-end, bedazzled handgun.

The people the Scarlet Mask and the Whisper had wanted to keep separate from the fight had followed them.

The militiamen did more harm than good, but they slowed the Whisper long enough for the Men of the Mask to arrive.

Each element operated two cyberpunk-inspired, electrically powered combat vehicles. Each vehicle carried three Men of the Mask: a driver, a team leader, and an operator. The vehicles could operate independently as three-man units or combine into six-man teams. They mounted twin 15mm machine guns.

The four vehicles from SDM and ACO reached the area first.

The Whisper drew a noxious gas grenade from one of his concealed pouches, using it to cover the civilians and himself. Then he scanned the electronic-warfare options in his augmented-reality helmet and selected an electro-psionic attack.

The attack struck the Men of ACO and SDM like needles driven behind their eyes and into their eardrums. They dropped, struggling against pain their armor could not stop.

Circuits had never been the strongest or fastest recruit. He had made it through Martian Colonial Marine Corps boot camp through sheer desire and esprit de corps. He refused to fail, and the Corps refused to let him. He had not been selected for Earth-gravity training or infantry. Poor physical-training scores had cost him a promotion. But Circuits was tough. He once completed a personal fitness test with a cracked kneecap; his run time was only slightly worse than usual. While the other Men of the Mask hit the ground, he stayed in the fight and linked the combat vehicles' weapons into air-defense mode.

If the Whisper tried to escape into the air, the vehicles could bring the overwhelming force of 15mm air-burst high-explosive rounds to bear. The civilians blocked any ground route of escape; at the same time, their presence shielded him from the Men of the Mask's precision fire as surely as armor. He readied himself for hand-to-hand combat.

Twofall was the most junior man on ACO-B, its operator, and had only recently left the MCMC. His family belonged to a small minority in Secondshima who saw the Scarlet Mask and the Men of the Mask as heroes: men trusted to step in where the government could not be trusted and the ideals of liberty failed. Minutes before the Whisper's attack, Twofall had been beaming with pride. By random chance, his Ready Rotation had placed him in the Scarlet Mask's tactical squad. He was proud; his family, most of his neighbors, and his friends would have been prouder still.

Now Twofall fought off the Whisper's attack. Red warning lights flashed across his AR combat helmet, indicating that the assault could take him out of the fight. He also knew the Whisper had to be stopped. The Men of the Mask were not fools. If they had chosen not to engage, the Whisper would have left, and their lives would not have been at risk. But the Whisper threatened Secondshima's liberty. To Twofall, that mattered more than their lives.

It felt as if someone were pouring magma into his skull. The Marines had prepared him for an attack, but not for how long each second could feel. He looked around his vehicle. He was the only one in ACO-B still in the fight. His first responsibility was to get SDM-B back in the fight, then SDM-A and the rest of ACO. The attack was striking the AR helmets directly.

Working with an AI, Twofall scoured the logs for the glitch the Whisper had found. The AI made the search lightning-fast; the molten pain in Twofall's head made it seem to take forever. The AI identified the source, but Twofall had to determine the fix. He ran it through the normal security pipeline. It went red.

*Fuck,* he thought. *They're down and out.*

The AI identified and fixed the security flaw, then restarted the pipeline while Twofall danced on the edge of consciousness. Five gates ran; all turned green. The lowest-ranked man in SDM-B would get the first live run. Twofall pushed deploy for the SDM-B operator, then let himself drift closer to unconsciousness for a moment.

Stanhoe was rare but not unusual among the Men of the Mask. He had not been a Martian Marine; he had been a Vostoshima police officer who got mixed up in drug running. He turned information over to the Scarlet Mask and was recruited and trained. Like the men on the SDM teams, he was pure Martian, with a long, lithe body efficient in Mars's low gravity and adapted to the lower pressure inside the domes.

Stanhoe lacked the strength and ruggedness of someone raised in full Earth gravity, like Quartermaster Washington, who had grown up on a space station with Earth-normal gravity and air pressure. In that moment, it did not matter. Stanhoe was on the ground, gunshots going off inside his head. He could not think, much less move.

Then the gunshots stopped. All that remained was a faint migraine, nothing a Man of the Mask could not endure. Stanhoe had one objective: disrupt the Whisper until reinforcements arrived. To protect the innocent, he could not fire his Martian Issue Service Rifle. It would have to become a bludgeon.

The Men of the Mask wore the same body armor as Martian Marines. It protected key areas against rifle rounds and other areas against pistol rounds and shrapnel. It also supported the joints, helping Martians operate on Earth while providing extra durability in a fight on Mars.

The Whisper's combat suit was the kind militaries spent fortunes developing, knowing full well it might never reach production: rifle plates everywhere, a faster, better-equipped AR helmet, and servos that made a lithe, native-born Martian stronger and faster than the average Earthman on Earth without sacrificing that remarkable Martian stamina on Mars.

Stanhoe's job was not to win a fight against the Whisper and that power suit. He could not. His job was to disrupt the Whisper until his team arrived. He sprinted, circled, and lunged, jabbing and jolting with his rifle. He wanted to be an annoying mosquito.

The Whisper swatted the mosquito. The blow sent Stanhoe flying. Mars's lighter gravity made the landing softer than it would have been on Earth or at MaSuLG4, but the blow had already done its damage.

Stanhoe was a Man of the Mask. He had broken ribs, a cracked forearm, and a punctured lung. He picked up his rifle and went back in.

Twofall sat in ACO-B. His code change had succeeded. He sent it to the rest of SDM-B and to ACO-A. Circuits processed the code through the proper security protocols, then Twofall uploaded it to the SDM-A operator.

Stanhoe's teammates quickly joined him. The fight looked like wolverines worrying a bear. The Whisper, in his powered suit and with elite hand-to-hand skills, was constantly harried by the Men of the Mask. They brought limited fighting skills, Martian stamina, and ferocity.

The two C&C vehicles sat just outside the fray. Their crews, unhelmeted for safety, were unaffected by the gas and watched the obscured figures fighting in the smoke. Washington stood between the vehicles, broad through the chest and shoulders, his close-cropped hair graying at the edges. His square face was usually calm, but now frustration had tightened it. He struck the side of one vehicle with his fist, then hit it again, unable to get through the attack and into the fight.

Stevie Tanaka stopped long enough to shed his overcoat and pull on the Scarlet Mask's combat helmet. Scarlet-and-gold tiger stripes cut across the hard-edged shell and visor housing. The sealed facepiece covered his features, its augmented-reality display and environmental filters built for smoke, dust, and combat. With the helmet on, he was no longer simply Stevie Tanaka wearing armor. He was the Scarlet Mask: a legacy made visible, and a commander moving toward his men.

He reached the C&C vehicles at a run and entered the electro-psionic attack at full force. The assault scrambled his thoughts. One imperative remained: get to his men. He crawled toward the noxious smoke until the men of C&C-A reached him, dragged him beyond the attack's range, and held him there until his head cleared.

The Whisper threw Stanhoe clear with another blow. The remaining men opened their attack from the front and rear. The Whisper snatched another man and hurled him toward Stanhoe. The SDM-B team leader judged that retreat was wiser than valor and moved to help his injured men.

Circuits' code had reached SDM-A, whose men were now clear-headed and back in the fight. All three applied the same pressure: constant and steady, but light. Their rifle-butt strokes, horizontal and smash, did not hurt the Whisper, but they annoyed him.

“We're green,” the Scarlet Mask called.

“C&C-B, bring that heavy muscle into the fight,” the Scarlet Mask ordered. The men of C&C-B had been born and raised mostly in Earth gravity. They had bigger, denser muscles and thicker bones. They would tire faster than the pure Martians, but they would hit harder.

“C&C-A, we have casualties,” the Scarlet Mask called. He and the other men of C&C-A moved through the smoke to the downed men of SDM-B. They switched the their AR helmets from combat mode to aid mode. Battlefield intel was replaced with first aid instructions. C&C-A began treating them.

The men of SDM-A and C&C-B worked together now, as they had been trained to do in MCMC boot camp and in the fleet. SDM-A maintained a relentless pace of distracting attacks. None were meant to injure; all were meant to annoy, harass, and distract. That created an opening for Quartermaster Washington to step in unobstructed.

Washington began with a clean rifle-butt stroke smash to the ribs.

Inside the Whisper's mask, something changed. Impact-event lights activated. Damage sensors flashed.

“Yeah! You felt that, motherfucker,” Washington said. “Your mama felt that.”

Washington and SDM-A fought the Whisper for about fifteen seconds. Then SDM-A increased the pressure. Washington stepped out to rest while another man from C&C-B took his place.

SDM-A and C&C-B fought the Whisper for nearly a minute. His noxious gas began to dissipate, and a crowd gathered, cheering each heavy blow landed by Washington and C&C-B. Some were fans of the Scarlet Mask. Others thought the Men of the Mask were criminals. But they were Secondshima's criminals.

Sometimes you stay in a fight long enough to get lucky. Just before a C&C-B fighter rotated in, a crack rang out, loud as a gunshot. The SDM-A team leader had landed a perfect blow, with perfect form, at the perfect time.

The Scarlet Mask, meters away and monitoring the wounded, snapped his head toward the sound. The Whisper's face shield was broken. At last, the Whisper and the Scarlet Mask stared each other down.

The Scarlet Mask did not see a staredown. He saw an image of himself split in two.

The Whisper was damaged and losing the fight. He had no choice but to risk it. He tucked and absorbed the blows long enough to reach his last-ditch option: a compressed-coil device. It launched a sturdy hook into a nearby building and pulled him into the shadows.

The guns on the SDM and ACO vehicles erupted with air-burst high-explosive fire. They were designed to hit targets moving at military speeds, but none of the rounds came close to the Whisper.
